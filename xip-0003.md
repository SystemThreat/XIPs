## Abstract

The consensus parameters of xCoin mainnet, frozen ahead of genesis on
**September 30, 2026**, and the ceremony that turns them into block 0. Every
value below is read from the public source tree
([github.com/SystemThreat/xCoin](https://github.com/SystemThreat/xCoin),
`src/kernel/chainparams.cpp` and `src/validation.cpp` at the commit this XIP
cites), not from anyone's memory. The genesis hash itself cannot appear here
yet — mining it IS the ceremony — so this XIP freezes everything that
determines it, and is amended once with the mined result.

either remove it or document it as unused, so no reader is misled.

## The ceremony

1. **Announce T.** The genesis time (planned: September 30, 2026) is announced
   in advance on minedifferent.com, xcoinproject.com, and this repository.
2. **Dress rehearsal first.** The full procedure below runs once on a
   short-lived rehearsal chain (test network identity, fresh genesis time,
   isolated ports) far enough ahead of T to mine through coinbase maturity and
   spend. No consensus change reaches mainnet unrehearsed (XIP-1).
3. **Mine block 0.** `xcoin-genesis -time=T` builds the charter genesis:
   coinbase committing the charter text, MetalDAG-mined to 0x1e0fffff.
4. **Freeze the result.** `FINAL_GENESIS_{MESSAGE,TIME,NONCE,BITS,HASH,MERKLE}`
   are filled in and `GENESIS_IS_FINAL` set true. Until this moment every
   shipped binary is identifiable but unlaunchable on mainnet — by design, so
   no premature chain can exist.
5. **The release.** Binaries are built from that commit and published as a
   GitHub release; their SHA-256 sums appear on at least three independent
   surfaces: the GitHub release page, xcoinproject.com, and superknet.com.
   The genesis hash is appended to this XIP, whose status then moves to
   Deployed.

## Activation

Chain: mainnet. Mechanism: genesis rule — the cleanest fork there will ever
be is the one at block 0 (XIP-1). Rehearsal: testnet A (live since September
2026, multi-country fleet plus independent community nodes) and the ceremony
dress rehearsal of step 2.

## Security considerations

The ceremony's threat is substitution: a different genesis presented as the
real one. Mitigations: T is announced beforehand; the charter text is fixed
and hash-committed; the constants land in a public commit anyone can rebuild;
and the release checksums live on three surfaces under different
infrastructure. The remaining trust is in the announcement channel itself —
which is why the sums go to three places, not one.
